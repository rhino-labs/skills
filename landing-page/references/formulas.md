# Headline formulas

The words in `[brackets]` are slots. Fill each slot with the specific words for this product, and highlight exactly those words in the accent color. Everything outside the brackets stays in the normal text color. This is how the wireframe's blue words work.

Draft several headlines from different formulas, then keep the most specific one. A formula that forces a vague slot ("[this ability]" → "work better") is the wrong formula for this product.

## Hero headline (`h1`)

**Product-led.** Use these when the audience already knows the category and is comparing options.

1. The [product] for [audience]
2. The [product] that enables [this ability] for [this audience]  *(the wireframe's default)*
3. The [product] for [audience] to [perform this task]
4. The [product] with [this differentiating feature]
5. The [product] that [performs this task] while you [enjoy this freedom/advantage]
6. The only [product] that can perform [ability one] and [ability two]
7. Imagine if [this product] could perform [ability one] and [ability two]

**Outcome-led.** Use these when the goal matters more to the reader than the category.

8. Everything that you need to [achieve this goal]
9. Achieve [this goal] without [this industry problem]
10. Perform [this task] in [hours] not [weeks]

**Pain-led.** Use these when the reader feels the problem but hasn't looked for a fix.

11. Don't suffer [this problem]. Enjoy [this ability].
12. Stop suffering from [this painful problem]
13. Finally, a [product] that does not have [this famous problem]

Whichever formula you use, the hero still has to tell the reader what the product is, what it does and who it's for. The kicker, explainer and checks carry whatever the headline leaves out. A pain-led headline needs a kicker that names the category.

## Pain points headline (`h2` in section 4)

This headline sums up the industry problem that the three problem cards add up to.

1. [Industry tools] claim to [perform this task] but they can't [handle this challenge]
2. You can't [perform this task] and it's [caused this problem]
3. Performing [this task] shouldn't [cause these problems]
4. [Audience] need a better way to [perform this task]
5. [Audience] experience [this problem] when they [perform this task]
6. Do you need a better way to [achieve these goals]?
7. Do you struggle to [achieve this goal] because of [this pain]?

Pick a formula that the three cards can answer. Formula 1 means each card should name a challenge the industry tools can't handle. Formula 5 means each card should name one way the problem shows up.

## Worked example (bookkeeping software for agencies)

| Formula | Headline |
| --- | --- |
| Hero 2 | The **ledger** that closes your **books in a day** for **growing agencies** |
| Hero 9 | Close **every month** without **losing a week to spreadsheets** |
| Hero 10 | Close **your books** in **hours** not **weeks** |
| Hero 13 | Finally, **bookkeeping software** that does not need **a finance team to run it** |
| Pain 1 | **Accounting tools** claim to **automate your books** but they can't **handle agency billing** |
| Pain 5 | **Agency owners** experience **a lost week every month** when they **close the books** |
